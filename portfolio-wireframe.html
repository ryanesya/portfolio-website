Problem identification
Back then, Amartha used to have the high-risk investment product.

Amartha provided a high-risk, high-return investment that could profit up to 15% p.a. — quite big compared to other products. But urban lenders faced the risk of losing money, which didn't match their medium-low risk profile, pushing them to invest elsewhere with lower risk (mutual funds, deposits).

We found that most of our urban lenders prefer low-risk investment.

In-depth interviews showed urban lenders mostly invest in low-risk instruments and worry about losing money — they treat their asset as savings rather than a profit vehicle.

Escalating to the grassroots segment, as the biggest segment.

For 15 years, Amartha has had 3.3 million grassroots users — the biggest segment in the company. Leveraging the investment product to this segment would be a massive growth opportunity.

Solution approach — research, benchmarking
Urban lenders: regularly investing, even with a "strategy."

They understand investment instruments and already invest regularly — for example, mutual funds for emergency needs, stocks for idle-money profit.

Grassroots: do not invest, but save regularly.

This segment saves weekly or monthly in places like arisan or koperasi, mostly toward a short-term goal (lebaran, children's education), in amounts of Rp10K–100K per month.

Highlighted flow — solves the core problem
Invest flow
  1. User comes from the homepage through the Celengan entry point.
  2. User chooses an offering catalog by investment goal, rate, or tenure preference.
  3. User sets the investment amount and selects an available tenure.
  4. User confirms the investment and gets the receipt.

Usability testing — test results

To ensure the proposed design works on both segments, we tested with both urban and grassroots users. Both segments could complete invest and divest easily, but three notable gaps came up:

FoundFix / status
Users wanted bulk divestment, but the system only supported one asset at a time.Logged as next iteration — not yet shipped.
Urban lenders wanted full catalog detail before entering an amount; grassroots users barely read it and just wanted to save.[to be added]
Urban lenders consider the catalog name (e.g. "Celengan Ternak Ayam") for its operational implications; grassroots users prefer goal-based names ("Celengan Lebaran").[to be added]
Summary / lessons learnt

What I'd carry forward

Designing for two personas with opposite mental models taught me to treat "shared vs. segmented flow" as an explicit trade-off, not an assumption.

Next iteration

User segmentation at the app level, and bulk divestment support (multi-asset or open amount).
